Suspicious-login investigation

I used sample authentication logs to investigate repeated failed logins and a successful login that followed them. The project helped me connect an alert to the evidence behind it.

  • Log analysis
  • Python
  • Investigation notes
Read project notes+

What I checked

The analyzer groups failed logins by IP and flags addresses with three or more failures. In the practice log, 203.0.113.42 had five failed attempts. I added a sample successful login from the same IP to explore a more concerning sequence.

What the finding means

The sequence is a reason to investigate, not proof of an account takeover. The logs are synthetic, and the IP is a documentation address used for this exercise.

What I took from it

Finding a pattern is the starting point. A useful investigation also separates observed events from interpretation and explains which account and session details would help confirm what happened.

Completed solo project · Synthetic practice investigation

02 / RISK & COMPLIANCECompleted · Solo
HARBOR & BEANFICTIONAL BUSINESS
01 Risk register02 Control mapping03 Acceptable use policy04 Vendor assessment

GRC Foundations

I wanted to see what GRC work looks like beyond definitions. I put together a set of security documents for a fictional Miami coffee business, with risks, owners, control gaps, and practical next steps.

  • Risk assessment
  • NIST CSF
  • Policy writing
Read project notes+

The scenario

Harbor & Bean Coffee Co. is a fictional Miami business with three café locations and an online store. Its small IT team needs a realistic way to prioritize security work.

What’s in the work sample

An eight-risk register with likelihood, impact, treatments, and owners; a control mapping across the six NIST CSF functions; an acceptable use policy; and a 20-question vendor assessment.

The part I care about

A risk score needs a reason behind it. Vendor answers also need evidence: a “yes” by itself doesn’t establish that a control is effective.

Completed solo project · Fictional business scenario

The repository includes a FastAPI backend, frontend configuration, backend tests, and Docker files. All 11 backend tests passed in my local check.

Why I built it

I wanted to connect what I learn about phishing with something people can interact with, and work through the full path from a security idea to a functioning project.

What I took from it
